Yuchen Kang

I am a third-year Ph.D. candidate in Mechanical Engineering at the University of Massachusetts Amherst and work with Prof. Gina Olson. My research focuses on soft robotics, compliant mechanisms, and mechanically intelligent structures. Before begining my Ph.D., I worked as a reasearch associate with Prof. Noah Jafferis and Prof. Mario Vallejo-Marin on "Robot Bees To Help Study Pollination" project. I received my Master's degree in Mechanical Engineering from Boston University, and conducted research in Morphable Biorobotics Lab with Prof. Tommaso Ranzani. I earned a Bachelor's degree in Engineering from Tianjin University of Science & Technology (TUST).

My work explores the design of soft robotic systems, pneumatic soft actuators, variable stiffness, and structural reinforcement for soft robots.

Buzz-pollinating bees deliver thoracic vibrations to flowers through periodic biting

Developed a bio-inspired robotic platform that mimics the periodic biting and thoracic vibrations of buzz-pollinating bees, enabling controlled investigation of how these vibrations are transmitted to flowers during pollination.

A multifunctional soft robot for cardiac interventions

Constructed a millimeter-scale soft robotic platform that can deploy and self-stabilize inside the beating heart, providing dexterous and stable guidance for minimally invasive cardiac interventions.

Numerical Simulation of Laser Drilling of BOPP Film Based on Birth and Death Element Method

Used the finite element method in ANSYS to investigate multipulse laser drilling of packaging films and assess the applicability of the element birth-and-death technique. Developed a two-dimensional transient temperature-field model in APDL and validated the simulation method through laser etching experiments.
